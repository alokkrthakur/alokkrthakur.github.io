This work addresses the limitations of well-mixed assumptions for indoor air pollution modeling in larger, multizonal spaces. PM2.5 and CO2 concentrations in different zones were predicted using conventional LSTM and physics-informed LSTM models based on measurements from one zone and different internal-door configurations. The models were evaluated using experimentally obtained spatiotemporal datasets, with exposure and maximum concentration relative to measured values used as performance metrics. The physics-informed model performed better for PM2.5 in most cases, while conventional LSTM showed better predictive accuracy for CO2.
